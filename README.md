# Mojo Pork Tacos

Slow-cooked mojo pork, shredded and then browned with onions, pork fat, and the concentrated cooking liquid.

## Ingredients

- 1 bone-in pork shoulder (pork butt/Boston butt), about 4 lb
- 1 bottle Goya Mojo Criollo marinade
  - **Use Goya; don't substitute Badia**
- 1 large onion

## Instructions

### 1. Slow-cook the pork

Place the pork shoulder in a slow cooker **fatty side up**.

Pour the entire bottle of Mojo Criollo marinade over the pork.

Cook on **LOW for 8 hours**.

### 2. Shred the pork

Carefully remove the pork from the slow cooker. It should be extremely tender and may fall apart as you remove it, so it's fine if it comes out in several pieces.

Allow the pork to cool enough to handle.

Remove and discard the bone and any unwanted pieces, then tear the pork into small pieces.

### 3. Save the cooking liquid

Pour all of the liquid remaining in the slow cooker into a large container and refrigerate until cool.

As it cools, it should separate into three layers:

1. **Pork fat** — floats to the top and solidifies.
2. **Pork-infused mojo** — the flavorful liquid in the middle.
3. **Seasoning sediment** — settles on the bottom.

Depending on how much gelatin is extracted from the pork bone, the middle layer may partially or completely solidify when refrigerated. That's perfectly fine.

### 4. Brown the pork and onions

Dice the onion.

Scoop some of the reserved pork fat into a large frying pan. You only need enough to fry the onions; discard the remaining fat or save it for another use.

Heat the pan over **medium heat** and begin frying the diced onion in the pork fat.

Add as much shredded pork as will comfortably fit in the pan **without overcrowding it**.

Allow the pork and onions to begin browning.

### 5. Add the mojo back

As the pork develops some color, spoon some of the reserved pork-infused mojo cooking liquid into the pan.

Allow the liquid to cook down into the meat.

Continue adding cooking liquid and letting it reduce until the pork has absorbed as much mojo flavor as you'd like and the pork-and-onion mixture has developed good browning.

Serve immediately.

## Notes

- Don't overcrowd the frying pan. Work in batches if necessary so the pork browns rather than steams.
- The reserved pork fat can be refrigerated and used for other cooking.
- Don't worry if the refrigerated cooking liquid turns into jelly. That's gelatin extracted from the pork and will melt again as soon as it's heated.
